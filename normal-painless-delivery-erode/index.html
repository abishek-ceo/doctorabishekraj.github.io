Normal delivery: why it is usually preferred

A normal (vaginal) delivery is a natural process that most healthy pregnancies can follow safely. Compared with a caesarean section, it generally means:

  • a quicker recovery and an earlier return to daily activities
  • a shorter hospital stay
  • a lower risk of surgical complications such as wound infection
  • an easier start to breastfeeding and skin-to-skin contact with your baby

It is not always possible, and that is fine. The goal is always a healthy mother and a healthy baby, by the safest route for you.

What is a painless (epidural) delivery?

An epidural is a form of pain relief for labour. An anaesthetist places a very thin tube in your lower back, close to the nerves that carry labour pain, and gives medicine through it. Within about 10 to 20 minutes the pain eases, while you stay awake, feel your contractions as pressure, and can push when it is time.

Good to know

  • It is widely used around the world and is generally considered safe for mother and baby.
  • It can be topped up during labour, so relief continues for as long as you need it.
  • Possible effects include a temporary drop in blood pressure, itching, shivering or numb legs. A headache afterwards is uncommon.
  • It does not usually increase the chance of a caesarean section, although the pushing stage may take a little longer.
  • It is not suitable for everyone, for example if you have a bleeding disorder or an infection at the injection site. Your doctor will check before recommending it.

Talk to Dr. Jayanthi Prabakaran about pain relief in the last trimester, so you can decide calmly, well before labour begins.

Normal delivery or caesarean?

A normal delivery is usually advised when…

  • your baby is head-down and growing well
  • your pregnancy is healthy, or a condition is well controlled
  • labour starts and progresses normally

A caesarean is advised when…

  • the baby is not getting enough oxygen, or labour is not progressing
  • the baby is lying sideways, or in some cases bottom-first
  • the placenta covers the cervix (placenta praevia)
  • there are certain medical conditions or previous uterine surgery

A caesarean can be lifesaving, and nobody should feel guilty about having one. We explain which route is safest for you and why, so you can decide together with your doctor.

Normal delivery after a previous caesarean

Some women who have had a caesarean can safely try for a normal delivery next time. This is known as VBAC (vaginal birth after caesarean). Whether it is suitable depends on why the earlier caesarean was done, the type of scar on your uterus, the gap between pregnancies and how this pregnancy is going. Dr. Jayanthi Prabakaran will assess you individually and explain the benefits and risks before you decide.

Preparing for a normal delivery

  • Keep up your antenatal visits and scans.
  • Stay active with daily walking and gentle exercise, unless your doctor has advised otherwise.
  • Practise breathing and relaxation, and learn the signs of labour: regular contractions that get stronger and closer together, your waters breaking, or a blood-stained show.
  • Eat well and stay hydrated.
  • Pack your hospital bag and keep your reports handy by 36 weeks.
  • Discuss pain relief, including the epidural, ahead of time.

Questions answered

Frequently asked questions

Is a painless delivery safe for my baby?
Epidural pain relief is widely used around the world and is generally considered safe for mother and baby. Only a small amount of medicine reaches the baby, and your baby is monitored during labour. Your doctor will check that it is suitable for you.
Will I feel anything with an epidural?
You will usually still feel pressure and tightening with contractions, and you can tell when to push, but the sharp pain is greatly reduced. The level of relief varies from woman to woman and can be adjusted.
Does an epidural increase the chance of a caesarean?
Studies show that epidural pain relief does not significantly increase the chance of a caesarean. It can make the pushing stage slightly longer.
Can I have a normal delivery after a caesarean?
Sometimes, yes. It depends on the reason for your earlier caesarean, your scar and your current pregnancy. Dr. Jayanthi Prabakaran will assess you and explain whether it is a safe option for you.
Do I have to decide about the epidural in advance?
No, you can ask for it during labour. But it helps to discuss it in the last trimester, so you know what to expect and any checks can be done in time.
