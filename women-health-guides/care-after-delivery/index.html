Care After Delivery: Recovery and Warning Signs

பிரசவத்திற்குப் பிந்தைய பராமரிப்பு

The weeks after birth are a time of healing. This guide covers what to expect, how to care for yourself and your baby, and the warning signs that need medical help.

What to expect in the first weeks

Bleeding after birth is like a heavy period at first and slowly lessens over about four to six weeks. Rest as much as you can, eat well, drink plenty of water and accept help at home. If you had stitches, keep the area clean and dry. After a caesarean, keep the wound dry, avoid heavy lifting and follow your doctor’s advice on movement and rest.

Warning signs: get help straight away

Call 97505 11225 or go to a hospital if you have:

  • very heavy bleeding (soaking a pad every hour) or large clots
  • fever, or foul-smelling vaginal discharge
  • a wound that is red, swollen, leaking pus or opening
  • severe pain in the tummy
  • a painful, swollen calf, breathlessness or chest pain
  • a severe headache with blurred vision or swelling
  • feeling very low or anxious, or thoughts of harming yourself or your baby

Emergency care is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. In a life-threatening emergency dial 108 or 112 for an ambulance.

Feeding your baby

Try to start breastfeeding within the first hour if you and the baby are well. Breastfeed only for the first six months, then add other foods and continue breastfeeding. If feeding hurts or the baby cannot latch, ask for help early: it usually improves with the right positioning.

Your mood and your check-up

Feeling tearful or low in the first two weeks (“baby blues”) is common. If it lasts longer, or you feel unable to cope, please talk to your doctor: help is available. Have a postnatal check-up about six weeks after delivery (earlier after a caesarean or complications) and discuss family planning, because breastfeeding does not reliably prevent pregnancy for long. Keep your baby’s vaccines on schedule.

Questions answered

Frequently asked questions

How long does bleeding last after delivery?
It lessens gradually over about four to six weeks. See a doctor if it suddenly becomes heavy, smells foul or comes with fever.
When can I start family planning?
You can discuss it at any time, and ideally at your six-week check-up. Please do not rely on breastfeeding alone to prevent pregnancy.
When will my caesarean wound heal?
The skin usually heals in a few weeks, but full recovery takes longer. Follow your doctor’s advice and avoid heavy lifting until you are told it is safe.
