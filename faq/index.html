Is emergency care available 24/7?
Yes. Call 97505 11225 at any hour or come straight to the hospital. In a life-threatening emergency, dial 108 or 112 for an ambulance.

Look for a registered obstetrician and gynaecologist with the qualifications and experience for the care you need, a hospital with emergency and delivery facilities available at all hours, clear and upfront pricing, and a doctor you feel comfortable talking to. Pregnancy and delivery

When should I first see a doctor in pregnancy?
As soon as you have a positive pregnancy test, ideally in the first trimester. Read about antenatal care.
Do you offer normal and painless delivery?
Yes. We support normal delivery, and epidural (painless) labour is available. Read about normal and painless delivery.
What makes a pregnancy high-risk?
Conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, twins, previous caesarean or pregnancy loss, thyroid disease or age under 18 or over 35. Read about high-risk pregnancy care.
Can I have a normal delivery after a caesarean?
Sometimes. It depends on your earlier surgery, your scar and your current pregnancy, and your doctor will assess you individually.

Gynaecology, PCOS and menopause

Which doctor should I see for period problems?
A gynaecologist. Irregular, heavy, painful or missed periods are common reasons to visit. Read about gynaecology care.
Is PCOS curable?
PCOS cannot be cured, but symptoms can be controlled very well with the right treatment and lifestyle changes. Read about PCOS treatment.
What is the difference between PCOD and PCOS?
The names are often used for the same problem in everyday talk. Doctors use PCOS, and the care is tailored to your symptoms and goals.
Do you offer menopause care and family planning?
Yes. We offer counselling and care for menopause and perimenopause, and personalised, affordable family planning and contraception advice.

Fertility and IUI

How long should we try before seeing a doctor?
Twelve months if the woman is under 35, and six months if she is 35 or older. Read about infertility treatment.
What is IUI?
Intrauterine insemination places specially prepared sperm in the uterus around ovulation. It is short, simple and does not need anaesthesia.
Does my husband also need tests?
Yes. A male factor contributes in about half of couples, and a semen analysis is a simple, painless first test.

Laparoscopic surgery

What is laparoscopic surgery?
Surgery through a few small cuts using a camera and fine instruments, with less pain, a shorter stay and faster recovery. Read about laparoscopic surgery.
Can fibroids and ovarian cysts be treated by keyhole surgery?
Often yes, depending on their size, number and position. Your doctor will advise you after an ultrasound.
