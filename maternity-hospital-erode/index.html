Antenatal care: what to expect

Good antenatal (pregnancy) care starts early. Book your first visit as soon as a home test is positive, ideally in the first trimester, so your due date and health can be checked and any risk can be caught early.

Your antenatal visits

Visits are usually about once a month until 28 weeks, every two weeks until 36 weeks, and weekly after that. Your doctor may ask you to come more often if your pregnancy needs closer watching.

Tests and scans

Your doctor will advise the blood tests, urine tests and ultrasound scans you need at each stage, for example:

  • blood group and Rh factor, haemoglobin and iron levels
  • blood sugar, to screen for gestational diabetes (usually between 24 and 28 weeks, earlier if you are at risk)
  • thyroid and infection screening, as advised
  • ultrasound scans to confirm the due date, check your baby’s development and monitor growth

Everyday care

Take folic acid, iron and calcium as prescribed, eat balanced home-cooked meals with enough protein, fruit, vegetables and water, walk daily unless told otherwise, and take the vaccines recommended in pregnancy. Avoid tobacco, alcohol and any medicine not prescribed for you.

Not sure of your due date? Use our free pregnancy due date calculator.

Bring to your first visit: previous medical and scan reports, a list of your medicines, the date of your last period and records of any earlier pregnancies.

Your delivery options

The safest way for your baby to be born depends on your health, your baby’s position and how labour progresses. Dr. Jayanthi Prabakaran will explain your options during your antenatal visits, so there are no surprises in labour.

Normal delivery

Usually the preferred route when it is safe for you and your baby: quicker recovery, a shorter hospital stay and an easier start to breastfeeding.

Painless (epidural) delivery

An epidural eases labour pain while you stay awake and in control. Over 2,500 deliveries with epidural pain relief have been completed.

Caesarean section, when needed

A C-section is advised when it is the safer option for you or your baby, for example in an emergency, or because of the baby’s position or a medical condition.

Warning signs: when to come in immediately

Call 97505 11225 or come to the hospital straight away if you have:

  • bleeding from the vagina, or leaking of water
  • your baby moving much less than usual
  • regular, painful contractions, especially before 37 weeks
  • a severe headache, blurred vision, or sudden swelling of the face and hands
  • severe or constant tummy pain
  • fever, burning while passing urine, or a fit (seizure)

Emergency care is available 24 hours a day. In a life-threatening emergency, dial 108 or 112 for an ambulance.

After the birth: postnatal care

Care does not end at delivery. In the first weeks you and your baby need support to recover and settle in.

  • Breastfeeding support. Exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months is recommended. Ask for help early if latching is painful or you are worried about your milk supply.
  • Your recovery. Care of stitches or the caesarean wound, bleeding, pain relief, rest and nutrition.
  • Your emotional health. Feeling low or tearful in the first two weeks is common. Tell your doctor if it lasts or feels severe.
  • Newborn care. Feeding, sleep, cord care, jaundice and the vaccination schedule.
  • Family planning. Safe ways to space your next pregnancy, discussed at your postnatal checkup, usually around six weeks after delivery.

Questions answered

Frequently asked questions

When should I first see a doctor in pregnancy?
As soon as you have a positive pregnancy test, ideally in the first trimester. Early visits let your doctor confirm your due date, check your health, start the right supplements and spot any risks early.
How many antenatal visits will I need?
Most women are seen about once a month until 28 weeks, every two weeks until 36 weeks, and weekly until delivery. If your pregnancy is high-risk, you may be seen more often.
Is emergency care available at night?
Yes. Emergency care at Abishek Hospital is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. If you are in labour or have a pregnancy emergency, call 97505 11225 or come straight to the hospital at Erode Fort.
Do you offer painless delivery?
Yes. Epidural (painless) labour is available, and over 2,500 deliveries with epidural pain relief have been completed. Learn more about painless delivery.
What should I bring to my first visit?
Your previous medical and scan reports, a list of medicines you take, the date of your last period, and records from earlier pregnancies. If you have diabetes, thyroid disease or high blood pressure, bring those reports too.
How much does delivery cost?
The cost depends on the type of delivery and any extra care you need. We explain expected costs clearly before treatment, with no hidden fees. Call or WhatsApp 97505 11225 to discuss your situation.
